# Legal Design Summit website brief for the next event cycle

Website audited: https://legaldesignsummit.com/index.html  
Audit date: 21 September 2026  
Purpose: Give the website-building AI a source-grounded content map, a proposed information architecture, and instructions for moving the existing website into a new event cycle.

## Instructions to the website-building AI

Build the next Legal Design Summit website around two connected purposes: helping people participate in the next Summit and supporting the ongoing international legal design community. Preserve the organisation’s identity, values, history, knowledge, and contributor recognition. Make the event information easy to find and maintain.

Use this brief together with the accompanying source archive. The archive contains extracted page text, page headings, links, image references, original HTML, 42 structured speaker profiles, and the original attendee-chart data. Read relevant source records before migrating their content. Do not reduce the site to a generic conference landing page or discard content simply because it currently sits inside a long page, expandable panel, chart, or script.

Treat the current website as predominantly a 2025 event website with some 2023 and earlier material. Its homepage now thanks attendees for 2025. Nothing in this audit establishes the next edition’s dates, venue, programme, partners, ticket terms, or team. Use an editable next-edition record with unconfirmed fields left empty. If the organiser selects 2027, apply that year through the edition record; do not infer exact dates or blindly replace “2025” with “2027”.

Separate four content states:

- **Evergreen:** organisational identity, purpose, values, history, and enduring resources. Retain with editorial review.
- **Historical:** event-specific programmes, speakers, workshops, metrics, partnerships, and practical arrangements. Preserve under the correct edition.
- **Needs confirmation:** information that could continue but requires an owner to verify it, such as team roles, email contacts, ticket rules, or venue arrangements.
- **Proposed:** new pages, features, and organisational choices suggested in this brief. These are recommendations, not claims about existing activities.

Never invent speakers, testimonials, sponsors, attendance figures, workshop capacities, policy terms, or registration links. Do not display a working-looking button unless its destination and availability have been confirmed. Preserve original names and diacritics; flag inconsistent spellings for review rather than silently merging people.

## Identity and enduring content

The website presents Legal Design Summit as a volunteer-led, international, non-profit legal design event. Legal Design Finland ry is identified as the organisation behind the Summit, BrainFactory, and associated side events. The policy gives business ID 3017417-5 and privacy@legaldesignsummit.com as the privacy contact. Preserve these as source facts and verify current controller details before publication.

The positioning connects law, design, business, and digital services with more understandable, human-centred legal services and better justice. The published values are **Selflessness, Inclusion, Courage, and Authenticity**. The history and blog also describe a wider community and movement, including systemic change, sustainability, education, and access to justice. The free-access model and volunteer contribution are central to the existing story; confirm next-edition ticket arrangements before making operational promises.

Preserve founder and volunteer recognition. The history describes the 2016 founding, international growth and first BrainFactory in 2017, transition to a non-profit and free event in 2019, pandemic interruption, the 2023 return, and work towards 2025. Do not recast past founding or board roles as current roles.

## Proposed new site structure

The following structure is a recommendation. It reorganises existing information and adds clearly identified entry points.

| Navigation group | Pages or destinations | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summit | Overview; Programme; Speakers; BrainFactory; Tickets; Plan your visit | Help visitors understand and attend the next confirmed edition |
| Community | Who attends; Volunteer; Partner with us | Explain participation and contribution; partnership page is new |
| Explore | Gallery; Blog; Past Summits | Keep knowledge and past editions discoverable between events |
| About | Our story; Values and code of conduct; Team and contact | Explain the organisation, responsibilities, and conduct expectations |
| Primary action | Register, join a confirmed waitlist, or view latest updates | Change with event phase and actual availability |
| Footer | Contact; social channels; reporting; privacy; recruitment privacy; archive links | Consistent essential information on every page |

Communities of practice, an ambassador programme, or a membership area are not established operational offerings in this audit. If desired, add them only after the organiser supplies a confirmed concept and content. Do not create invented testimonials, membership benefits, or active applications.

### Recommended homepage sequence

1. Clear identity, next-edition status, confirmed city/dates when available, and one primary action.
2. A short explanation of legal design and who the Summit serves.
3. Ways to participate: Summit sessions, BrainFactory, volunteering, and partnerships.
4. Confirmed programme and speaker highlights, or honest release-status text.
5. Community evidence with year-labelled metrics and attributed or source-preserved testimonials.
6. Gallery projects and selected blog stories.
7. Current confirmed supporters and collaborators, with historical support separately labelled.
8. Latest updates or a verified subscription option, then the shared footer.

Give BrainFactory, the Gallery, and volunteer participation visible entry points. Avoid forcing all content onto the homepage. Do not show empty carousels, placeholder people, or dead “coming soon” buttons.

## Event lifecycle and publishing rules

| Phase | Public behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Between editions | Show the latest recap and enduring resources; state the next edition’s status without inventing dates |
| Announcement | Publish confirmed date/location and participation information; provide a real update route if configured |
| Recruitment and proposals | Publish only approved calls and forms with deadlines, eligibility, review process, and appropriate privacy information |
| Registration | Enable verified booking; show complete ticket terms, workshop dependencies, cancellation, and waitlist instructions |
| Programme release | Publish confirmed sessions and speakers progressively; label partial programmes accurately |
| Event week | Prioritise schedule, venue, access, changes, contacts, conduct/reporting, and practical information |
| Post-event | Close booking and applications; publish thanks, approved media/resources, and feedback routes; preserve edition pages |

Changing phase must not overwrite history. Copy structure into a new edition, then populate confirmed content. Old editions retain their original programmes and credits with a clear past-event label.

## Issues to resolve before launch

- Homepage says 10 BrainFactory workshops; the workshop page contains nine and the BrainFactory blog also describes nine. Retain both source claims in the audit and obtain an approved figure.
- Homepage says 50+ expert speakers; the speaker data contains 42 profile records. These may cover different populations, such as facilitators as well as stage speakers. Confirm scope rather than treating either as automatically wrong.
- The attendee page labels 600+ attendees in 2023, but chart values total 161 and displayed percentages total 100. The denominator and coverage are unexplained. Do not present the chart as a full breakdown of all 600+ attendees without clarification.
- `/program-thursday.html` and `/speakers-thursday.html` returned 404. Replace internal references and redirect old paths to the matching historical content, not automatically to the newest edition.
- Speaker cards construct links using `/program#…`, while the main programme is `/program.html`. Verify route handling and matching session anchors in the rebuild.
- The homepage contains partner/collaborator links that are `#`, point back to LDS, or use a bare `legaldesignsummit.com` relative path. Some image alt text is missing or mismatched. Check the full asset/link manifest before migration.
- Travel content refers to late-autumn weather despite the September 2025 event context. Confirm seasonally appropriate advice.
- General privacy is marked updated 9 April 2023. The new website’s forms, embedded services, providers, and actual processing must be reviewed against both privacy notices. This audit preserves policy content; it does not certify legal compliance.
- Contacts, team membership, code-of-conduct reporting responsibility, event claims, and image permissions need an editorial owner’s confirmation.

## Acceptance criteria for the rebuilding AI

- Every discovered source page has a destination or an explicitly approved archive treatment.
- Both programme days, all nine workshops, all 42 speaker records, all eight articles, and all eight Gallery projects are accounted for.
- Complete biographies, project details, acknowledgements, historical volunteer recognition, and policy sections survive migration; they are not replaced by card summaries alone.
- Dates, status, CTAs, and confirmation rules are consistent across all pages. No expired 2025 registration flow is presented as current.
- Programme entries connect to the correct people and edition; workshop facilitators remain distinguishable from other appearances.
- Navigation, menus, filters, disclosures, forms, and timetable views work with keyboard and mobile use. Use clear headings, readable text, contrast, meaningful alt text, and text alternatives for charts.
- The Gallery and blog remain usable when third-party social embeds fail. Prefer optional embeds over dependencies for essential content.
- Redirects preserve old page and section links where feasible. Publish a sitemap and metadata for approved pages. Only mark confirmed real event details in structured data.
- Booking, recruitment, subscription, and reporting actions connect to verified destinations. Do not collect personal data through mock forms.
- The final handover includes a populated content inventory, redirects, outstanding confirmations, and instructions for changing event phase and creating the next edition.
